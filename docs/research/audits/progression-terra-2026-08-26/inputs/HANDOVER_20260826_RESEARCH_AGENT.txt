## 0. What this document is, and what it deliberately omits

This is a **state-of-the-question** handover, not a state-of-the-code handover.

It tells you which questions are already closed, which were already answered by an earlier
review, which are open and waiting on evidence, and what happens to your report when it lands.

**It contains no file paths, no line numbers, and no code.** That is deliberate. The brief you
are working from instructs you to ignore the repository, because the code available to you is a
different revision and does not match. Handing you a code map alongside that instruction would
invite exactly the confusion the instruction exists to prevent. If you find yourself wanting to
check something in code, the answer is that the brief is the specification — and if the brief is
silent on something, that silence is itself an answer worth reporting.

## 1. The one governance rule that shapes everything

The project operates under a ratified **Calibration Policy** (owner-ratified 2026-08-14).

Its core provision: **no numerical value enters the engine without the owner's explicit
ratification, recorded against a source.** There is a frozen registry of every coefficient
currently in the system. No coefficient may be inferred from a movement's name, its equipment
label, its movement pattern, or its similarity to another movement. Values that have not been
ratified are deliberately absent from the code rather than filled with a plausible default.

Three consequences for your report:

1. **Your findings inform a ratification decision. They do not authorise anything.** Nothing you
   write becomes a value in the product without a separate, explicit human ruling.
2. **A number stated confidently in a research report is the primary vector by which unsourced
   values get into systems like this one.** The owner is aware of that and is reading for it. A
   value you cannot source is worse than no value, not better.
3. **"The evidence does not support a specific value" is a fully useful result.** The engine
   already has several deliberately empty slots that are waiting for exactly that verdict, and
   an honest blank lets the owner stop looking. A plausible fill makes him stop looking for the
   wrong reason.

## 3. Already answered — a companion review covered these

An earlier evidence review answered eight questions. **Do not re-answer them.** Where your work
touches one, note the overlap in a single line and move on.

1. Whether acute:chronic workload ratio should drive a readiness score.
2. What telemetry would actually change prescription for combat-sport athletes.
3. Whether per-muscle weekly volume landmarks (minimum effective / maximum adaptive / maximum
   recoverable volume) are supportable.
4. How competition peaking and tapering should work.
5. Whether linear / undulating / autoregulated is a sufficient set of loading schemes.
6. What should determine which loading scheme an athlete receives.
7. Whether step loading is a distinct method.
8. Whether conjugate is a block-organisation model rather than a loading curve.

Question 3 above is the closest neighbour to your Q3. The distinction: theirs asked whether
per-muscle volume *landmarks* are supportable. Yours asks whether volume-load *equates across
load and repetition configurations*. Those are different questions and yours is not answered by
theirs.

## 4. Deliberately absent numbers — these are what you are actually for

The following values do not exist anywhere in the system, by policy, because no cited source
supplies them for this population. Each is blocking a decision:

- **How large a change in estimated strength is real rather than measurement noise.** This is the
  single most load-bearing gap. Without it, no trend can be called progress or stagnation, and a
  whole class of features cannot be built. Your Q2 and Q5 bear directly on it.
- **How long a non-improving trend must persist before it means anything.**
- **What a taper does** — its duration, and its volume reduction. When a taper should happen is
  now derivable; how much it changes is not.
- **How far out from a competition date preparation should begin.**
- **How many blocks a training phase should last.**
- **Any load or effort modifier for entering or leaving a rehabilitation state.**

If the literature does not support one of these, saying so plainly closes the question. That is a
successful outcome, not a failed one.

## 5. Facts about the system that are easy to get wrong

These have caused confusion before. Each is stated in the brief; they are repeated here because
they are the ones most likely to be assumed away.

- **The field the app calls "session RPE" is not Foster's session-RPE.** It is the arithmetic
  mean of the logged per-set ratings, rounded to the nearest half point. It is never a single
  whole-session rating and is never multiplied by duration. If your answer discusses session-RPE
  as the literature uses the term, say explicitly that the app's field of that name is a
  different quantity.
- **A bodyweight set contributes exactly zero to the app's volume figure**, because that figure
  is repetitions multiplied by external load. This is a known and accepted limitation, not an
  oversight, and the app is forbidden from claiming the figure represents total mechanical work.
- **The app receives no data at all from grappling or conditioning sessions.** It schedules them
  and hears nothing back — no duration, no rounds, no intensity. Any load or progression figure
  it computes is therefore built on systematically incomplete data.
- **The estimated-1RM calculation exists but nothing consumes it.** It is not stored, not
  displayed, and does not influence any prescription. It carries no noise floor and no definition
  of a stall, deliberately, because those are unratified numbers.
- **There is no measurement of repetition velocity.** A field for it exists and nothing writes to
  it. Treat velocity-based methods as requiring hardware the app does not have.
- **There is no per-muscle volume tracking of any kind.**
- **There is no coach.** Nobody verifies an RPE rating, notices technique breakdown, or
  intervenes on a bad day. This is the constraint most likely to break the transfer of a protocol
  validated on supervised trained subjects, and it should be addressed explicitly wherever it
  matters rather than noted once.

## 6. No audience data exists

No product has launched. There is **no age range, no training-age distribution, no confirmed
sport mix, and no usage data.** Do not assume any.

What the design implies, and no more: training ages from beginner through elite; one to seven
sessions per week; grappling represented as a first-class training day. Grappling is the only
combat sport modelled — there is no striking, wrestling or mixed-martial-arts handling. Where
evidence exists for striking or mixed martial arts but not grappling, say which population you
actually found.

## 7. Where your report lands

The product is in a pre-release closed-beta pipeline. It is feature-complete enough to test and
is being prepared for closed testing on Android.

Your report does not gate that release. It feeds the decision about **what the app measures and
reports as progress**, which is currently one-dimensional and which the owner believes is wrong.
That work has not started and will not start until the evidence is in — which is why an honest
blank is genuinely actionable here and a plausible fill is genuinely damaging.

The report will be read against the evidence standard in Part 2 of the brief: every factual claim
tiered inline, every claim carrying a digital object identifier or PubMed identifier, population
transfer stated, effect size and uncertainty given, and commercial systems separated from the
science they borrow.

Two structural requirements from Part 4 that are easy to miss on a first read:

- Each question section ends with a short block separating findings solid enough to decide on
  from things that are convention or practitioner consensus.
- The report closes with an explicit list of **every number a reader might mistake for a
  ratified recommendation**, each with its tier and its population.

And the one prohibition: **do not design a progression score.** No formula, no weighting, no
composite index, no thresholds. If the evidence supports a multi-dimensional model, name the
dimensions and say what measures each — and stop. Composition is the owner's ratification
decision, and a plausible-looking formula in your report would pre-empt it.
