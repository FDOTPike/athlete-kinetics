## Precedence proposal

This ordering is product policy proposed for ratification, not a claim that a guideline prescribes this exact algorithm.

1. Current applicable clinician restrictions and clinically reviewed screening/stop conditions constrain the permitted recommendation domain. No goal, tier, preference, calendar commitment or favorable readiness value overrides them. If applicability is unknown, hold affected personalized advice until clarified.
2. Current recovery/symptom information and actual recent activity constrain new work. An unexpectedly hard session or worsening symptoms suppresses proposed increases; no arbitrary new reduction percentage or sport recovery interval is invented.
3. Preserve fixed existing commitments as calendar facts. Fit optional coach work into user-confirmed available time; expose conflicts and offer edits. A restriction conflicting with sport does not move sport or mark participation safe: show the incompatibility and hold the affected advice.
4. Preferences and goals choose among feasible options. Experience helps select permissible movements and explanations but cannot supply additional workload or a higher physiological budget by itself.
5. When more than one feasible suggestion remains, use a stable deterministic order and explain the decisive inputs. Store proposal/policy version and input provenance so an offline user can understand why it changed.

If time is insufficient, suggest less/no additional coach work rather than borrowing time from a fixed activity. If no supported prescription can meet the constraints, give a clear unresolved status and keep history and non-prescriptive activity capture available. A blank medical field must not silently mean absence of restrictions.

## Restriction composition examples for review

These are input fixtures, not recommendations or default medical limits.

- Two confirmed maximum-HR limits applying to the same activity and period can be compared only after compatible units/operators are established; the stricter maximum remains a candidate effective ceiling. Keep both sources visible and flag disagreement for review. Conflicting minimum/maximum ranges or uncertain applicability hold advice; do not average limits or assume the latest entry automatically wins.
- A user enters “stay below 150 bpm.” The prose alone executes nothing. A confirmed structured ceiling with the intended operator is required. Proposed boundary display for an inclusive maximum: warn upon receiving a sample at the ceiling; report exceeded only above it. If the clinician instruction is strictly below the value, the boundary itself violates it. This operator distinction, warning wording and timing require clinical/native review; 150 is never a default.
- A new instruction supersedes an older one only through an explicit reviewed relationship and effective period. Deleting a note does not silently delete a separate structured limit. Deleting health data is supported but must make the resulting unknown screening/restriction state truthful; it must not create verified clearance.
- A general “seated preferred” setting can help filter choices where supported. Unknown transfer requirements or symptoms are not resolved by replacing standing with sitting. POTS escalation and fluid/salt advice remain individualized; this work adds no treatment schedule.

## Accounting and time acceptance examples

Fixture values below are chosen to expose behavior, not prescribe exercise.

| Fixture | Expected accounting/behavior |
|---|---|
| Swim planned 30 min at effort 4, completed 25 min at effort 5 | Actual view shows 25 min and rough 125 arbitrary units; projected view uses that actual once. Original plan remains 30 min/120 units for comparison, never added to actual. |
| Planned swim cancelled; no actual record | Completed minutes/load do not increase. Remove its expected work from the active projected plan; do not automatically fill freed time with harder coach work. |
| Same completed workout recorded by coach session and manual activity | Link both records to one canonical occurrence or request explicit duplicate resolution. Components may describe strength sets and activity time, but the parent session duration contributes once. |
| Friday sport at 17:00 in the series timezone through a DST change | Local commitment remains at 17:00; resolved UTC time may change. Both engine input and displayed reason identify the timezone. No fixed-offset-only recurrence. |
| Sunday activity crosses into Monday | Split known interval minutes at local midnight/week boundary; keep a single occurrence and single actual record. Unknown timestamps leave day allocation uncertain. |
| Repeated/missing DST local time | Display unresolved time and require explicit choice; no invisible offset change. Preserve chosen resolution and original recurrence identity. |
| Unknown custom hobby with duration but no effort or impact answer | Save normally; count known time, show unknown effort/load/demand. Do not allocate exact muscle stress or infer exercise suitability. |
| Fixed sport overlaps a confirmed clinician restriction | Preserve calendar fact, flag incompatible advice, hold affected prescription. A fixed flag never outranks the restriction. |
| Unexpected hard session, current recovery unknown | Explain the new input, suppress any proposed increase and request recovery/plan review. Do not invent a mandatory recovery-day count. |

Actual totals, planned totals and projected totals must be separately labelled. Public-health aerobic minutes, strength exposure, and rough duration-effort units are distinct ledgers of the same occurrence, not interchangeable “work credits.” Overlapping distinct activities are flagged; a known interval union can describe occupied time, but physiological load is not automatically merged or split by overlap.
