## E01 — WHO adult public-health guidance

- Source/date: [WHO Guidelines on Physical Activity and Sedentary Behaviour](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240015128), 25 November 2020; [original guideline recommendations hosted by NCBI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK566046/), adult and disability sections. Both resolved.
- Population: adults, with separate recommendations for older adults, chronic conditions and disability. Applicability depends on function and health status.
- Outcome: population health benefits associated with regular activity. Adult reference: 150–300 moderate aerobic minutes/week or 75–150 vigorous minutes, or an equivalent combination; muscle strengthening on at least 2 days/week.
- Limitations: health reference ranges do not identify a universal first week, optimal sport preparation, weight-loss dose, or clinician clearance. Small amounts are useful; individual activity and tolerance matter.
- Classification/confidence: published public-health guidance; high confidence for educational scope, insufficient for individual starting dose.
- Exact proposed app effect: an optional offline long-term reference card; count known appropriate activity toward its relevant domain. Never auto-fill the lower bound as a new user's week. No minimum bout length imposed for descriptive activity logging.
- Review: 2027-03-12, sooner if WHO updates or scope expands.

## E02 — Current resistance-training statement

- Source/date: [Currier et al., ACSM position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/), online 5 March 2026; issue 1 April 2026, 58(4):851–872. [ACSM official announcement](https://acsm.org/science-spotlight-acsm-releases-new-position-stand-on-resistance-training/) confirms the update. PubMed abstract and official announcement resolved; the publisher DOI intermittently errored and PMC presented a browser challenge, so this review does not claim complete full-text appraisal.
- Population: healthy adults in systematic reviews of randomized resistance-training trials lasting at least six weeks; not a clinical POTS/disability prescription study.
- Outcome: muscle function, hypertrophy and physical performance; this updates the 2009 progression statement.
- Limitations: an overview of reviews does not validate this app's precise sets, effort waves, experience multipliers, fatigue budgets or first-session safety.
- Classification/confidence: official professional guidance/evidence synthesis; high confidence about its scope and currency, moderate for transfer to general coaching, insufficient for app-specific constants.
- Exact proposed app effect: make this the current resistance-training review checkpoint; do not justify inherited numeric algorithms by citing the 2009 statement alone. Require full-text appraisal before a new goal-specific resistance recipe is ratified.
- Review: 2027-03-12; full-text clinical/programming appraisal before new resistance defaults.

## E03 — Weight outcomes are not mechanically predictable

- Source/date: [Church et al., Changes in weight, waist circumference and compensatory responses](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19223984/), 2009, PLoS One 4(2):e4515. Original study abstract resolved.
- Population: 411 sedentary, overweight/obese postmenopausal women in an analysis of a supervised randomized exercise trial.
- Outcome: actual versus predicted weight change and waist circumference over six months; greater exercise dose did not translate mechanically into predicted weight loss.
- Limitations: selected population, supervised setting, secondary analysis; no personalized weight prediction or universal optimal dose established.
- Classification/confidence: primary research; high confidence against a guarantee, limited generalization of numerical outcomes.
- Exact proposed app effect: explain that activity supports health and goals but weight outcomes vary. Do not derive kilograms lost, calories owed, or extra sessions from a weight-loss goal.
- Review: 2027-03-12.

## E04 — Early observational training-load method

- Source/date: [Foster, Monitoring training in athletes with reference to overtraining syndrome](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9662690/), July 1998, 30(7):1164–1168. Original abstract resolved.
- Population: 25 experienced athletes.
- Outcome: associations of training characteristics, including session effort and duration, with illness observations.
- Limitations: small observational sample; individually identified associations cannot establish a universal injury, illness or recovery threshold. No threshold from this study is adopted.
- Classification/confidence: primary observational research; moderate confidence for monitoring rationale, low for causal/clinical prediction.
- Exact proposed app effect: retain activity history and self-reported recovery as context. Never label a load ratio, monotony value or accumulated score as clinically safe.
- Review: 2027-03-12.

## E05 — Session duration multiplied by perceived effort

- Source/date: [Foster et al., A new approach to monitoring exercise training](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11708692/), February 2001, 15(1):109–115; [original paper PDF copy](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/A%20New%20Approach%20to%20Monitoring%20Exercise%20Training.pdf), methods pp. 110–112. Bibliographic identity and original paper resolved; PDF host is a third-party copy, not a publisher endorsement.
- Population: 12 trained recreational cyclists and 14 male collegiate basketball players.
- Outcome: session-RPE-derived load tracked a heart-rate method, with different absolute values. The method multiplies session minutes by session effort.
- Limitations: small selected samples, subjective scale, recording delay and duration definition matter. Equal products need not mean equal tissue stress, impacts, fatigue or adaptation. Unknown hobbies/adaptive activities and clinical safety were not validated.
- Classification/confidence: primary measurement research; moderate for rough individual descriptive monitoring; insufficient for cross-sport substitution or clinical prediction.
- Exact proposed app effect: optional `minutes × whole-session effort` in arbitrary units, showing inputs and missingness. A custom 1–10 UI is an adaptation, not an identical validated instrument. Missing effort gives unknown load; no default effort, thresholds or load-based automatic escalation.
- Review: 2027-03-12; scale/protocol review before implementation.

## E06 — Challenge to a universal progression percentage

- Source/date: [Buist et al., No effect of a graded training program on running-related injuries](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17940147/), online 16 October 2007; January 2008, 36(1):33–39. Original randomized-trial abstract resolved.
- Population: 532 novice runners preparing for a recreational running event.
- Outcome: a 13-week program applying a 10% rule did not reduce running injuries versus an eight-week standard program.
- Limitations: one running comparison; does not prove all gradual progression ineffective or establish another safe percentage.
- Classification/confidence: primary randomized research; high confidence against treating 10% as a universally protective law; limited sport transfer.
- Exact proposed app effect: no universal weekly percentage increase or claim that staying below it prevents injury. Any later progression rate needs its own population-specific rationale and ratification.
- Review: 2027-03-12.

## E07 — Resistance-specific RPE/RIR

- Source/date: [Zourdos et al., Novel Resistance Training-Specific Rating of Perceived Exertion Scale Measuring Repetitions in Reserve](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26049792/), January 2016, 30(1):267–275. Original abstract resolved.
- Population: 29 experienced and novice squatters, mean age approximately 24 years.
- Outcome: a resistance-specific RIR-based scale was related to movement velocity; experience affected reports near maximum effort.
- Limitations: squat testing, small sample; not evidence that global session effort, cardiovascular intensity or a swimming effort can be converted into repetitions remaining.
- Classification/confidence: primary measurement research; moderate within resistance context, insufficient outside it.
- Exact proposed app effect: preserve scale identity and measurement scope. Strength-set RIR-derived values may retain their explicit conversion; a whole-session activity effort must be stored separately. Never apply `RIR = 10 − effort` to sport-session entries.
- Review: 2027-03-12.

## E08 — Individualized POTS care

- Source/date: [Johns Hopkins Medicine: POTS](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/conditions-and-diseases/postural-orthostatic-tachycardia-syndrome-pots), publication/update date not stated on retrieved page; checked 2026-09-12. Original provider guidance resolved; exercise and symptoms sections reviewed.
- Population: people with POTS, with variable symptoms, functional ability and comorbidities.
- Outcome: describes individualized treatment and tolerance-based exercise; activity can worsen symptoms, including prolonged worsening. Reclined options may be considered in individual care.
- Limitations: patient guidance, not a validated app algorithm or individualized medical order; no universal workload, heart-rate ceiling, fluid or salt prescription follows.
- Classification/confidence: official clinical-provider guidance; high confidence for the need to individualize, insufficient for numerical automatic treatment.
- Exact proposed app effect: capture functional preferences and clinician instructions, make symptom worsening a reason for review, and hold automatic progression while restrictions are unresolved. Offer no default POTS heart-rate, fluid or salt targets. A positional preference alone does not establish movement suitability.
- Review: 2026-10-12 and qualified clinical review before medical adaptation.

## E09 — Australian adult screening candidate

- Source/date: [ESSA/SMA/Fitness Australia APSS tool](https://www.essa.org.au/Common/Uploaded%20files/Tools%20and%20templates/Adult%20Pre%20Screening%20Tool%202021.pdf), document marked V2 (2019), despite 2021 in filename; [official access page](https://www.essa.org.au/Public/ABOUT_ESSA/Adult_Pre-Screening_Tool.aspx?WebsiteKey=b4460de9-2eb5-46f1-aeaa-3795ae70c687). Tool PDF resolved.
- Population: adults before exercise; separate systems exist for young people and pregnancy.
- Outcome: flags need for appropriate professional guidance from disease/symptom questions and recent activity.
- Limitations: screening does not guarantee safety. Its weighted activity calculation includes light activity and is not interchangeable with WHO moderate-equivalent minutes. Copying only selected questions would create a new unvalidated screen.
- Classification/confidence: professional screening guidance; high confidence about tool content, clinical review required for app routing and scope.
- Exact proposed app effect: recommend this as the Australian review starting point; clinician decides applicable screen and exact triggers before execution. Persist answers/version/state, not an inferred diagnosis or verified clearance. No APSS threshold is adopted as weekly prescribing logic.
- Review: 2026-10-12, before screening implementation.

## E10 — ACSM screening comparator

- Source/date: [Riebe et al., Updating ACSM's Recommendations for Exercise Preparticipation Health Screening](https://journals.lww.com/acsm-msse/fulltext/2015/11000/updating_acsm_s_recommendations_for_exercise.28.aspx), November 2015, 47(11):2473–2479. Original professional statement resolved.
- Population: adults considering/undertaking exercise; cardiovascular, metabolic and renal disease/symptom context.
- Outcome: routes using current activity, known disease/signs/symptoms and intended intensity, aiming to reduce unnecessary referral barriers.
- Limitations: not a universal disability or POTS screen; historical guidance needs qualified review against current practice. Its original RPE intensity labels use a different scale from this app's proposed 1–10 effort.
- Classification/confidence: professional consensus guidance; moderate confidence for design principles, insufficient for a new clinical algorithm.
- Exact proposed app effect: avoid disability-label-only exclusion, and retain existing activity/intended intensity in the screening contract. Compare with E09 during clinical review; do not combine their numeric branches into an invented hybrid screen.
- Review: 2026-10-12, before screening implementation.

## E11 — Recurrence and timezone identity

- Source/date: [IETF RFC 5545](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc5545/), September 2009; sections 3.3.5, 3.6.5, 3.8.4.4 and 3.8.5. Official specification resolved.
- Population/outcome: calendar implementations; stable recurring-instance identity and unambiguous time semantics.
- Limitations: a calendar standard does not choose clinical, duration-accounting or app UX policy. Current timezone rules require a suitable offline timezone implementation.
- Classification/confidence: primary technical standard; high confidence for identity/time principles, product decisions remain separate.
- Exact proposed app effect: stable activity/series/occurrence IDs, original recurrence key preserved on rescheduling, local wall time plus timezone for recurrence and resolved instants for occurrences. Explicit ambiguity handling. No runtime calendar service is required.
- Review: 2027-03-12; timezone dependency/rule-data review during implementation.

## H01 — Existing app constants: product heuristics, not external evidence

Source/date: audited repository at the frozen baseline, checked 2026-09-12. Population: users of the current general training engine; a validation population for clinical safety is not established. Outcome: deterministic software behavior and passing gates; these do not measure health outcomes. Confidence: high about inspected code behavior, insufficient about clinical validity. Classification: inherited product heuristics. Review: Francis/programming review before these constants inform WO-04/05 recommendations; clinical review for symptoms/restrictions.

Exact proposed effect: preserve baseline during this documentation work; do not rebrand the defaults as evidence-backed. The [legacy numeric inventory](WO04_PERSONA_POLICY_AUDIT.md#legacy-numeric-inventory) supplies exact code/test locations for every inherited number discussed. Ratification history may explain why a value shipped; it does not supply external efficacy evidence.
