# MOV-A15 Draft Descriptions for the Five Source-Hold Movements

This document records the source-bound draft descriptions (`instructions`, `cues`, and `coachingIntent`) for the five catalogue movements on a source hold (`145`, `153`, `165`, `205`, `250`). Each draft is written from the public-domain `free-exercise-db` entry (Unlicense) and its two photographs, resolves every hold reason in `ALL_300_MOTION_MAP.json` and `MOV-A15.md`, and explicitly marks as **a choice** any detail that the source text and photographs do not settle. The catalogue entries themselves are not modified.

---

## 1. Movement `145` — `Bent Over Two-Arm Long Bar Row` (`bent-over-two-arm-long-bar-row`)

### Hold Reason & Family Constraints
- **`MOV-A15.md` hold reason**: `the long-bar row set-up (pivot, handle, anchor) is not defined`
- **`ALL_300_MOTION_MAP.json` `motion.source_hold_reason`**: `"Barbell plus cable-machine equipment conflicts with unspecified long-bar row setup; define pivot/handle/anchor."`
- **`ALL_300_MOTION_MAP.json` `motion.family_constraints`**: `"Quiet hinged torso; arms pull elbows back then regain long reach; implement, supinated/neutral grip and landmine pivot are explicit per-ID source requirements."`

### Public-Domain Source Quotations & Photographs
- **JSON Source URL**: `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/main/dist/exercises.json` (entry `"id": "Bent_Over_Two-Arm_Long_Bar_Row"`)
- **Word-for-Word `instructions`**:
  1. `"Put weight on one of the ends of an Olympic barbell. Make sure that you either place the other end of the barbell in the corner of two walls; or put a heavy object on the ground so the barbell cannot slide backward."`
  2. `"Bend forward until your torso is as close to parallel with the floor as you can and keep your knees slightly bent."`
  3. `"Now grab the bar with both arms just behind the plates on the side where the weight was placed and put your other hand on your knee. This will be your starting position."`
  4. `"Pull the bar straight up with your elbows in (to maximize back stimulation) until the plates touch your lower chest. Squeeze the back muscles as you lift the weight up and hold for a second at the top of the movement. Breathe out as you lift the weight. Tip: Use a stirrup or double handle cable attachment by hooking it under the end of the bar."`
  5. `"Slowly lower the bar to the starting position getting a nice stretch on the lats. Tip: Do not let the plates touch the floor. To ensure the best range of motion, I recommend using small plates (25-lb ones) as opposed to larger plates (like 35-45lb ones)."`
  6. `"Repeat for the recommended amount of repetitions."`
- **Photographs**:
  - `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/main/exercises/Bent_Over_Two-Arm_Long_Bar_Row/0.jpg`: Side-oblique view of the athlete straddling an Olympic barbell. The unloaded rear end of the bar is anchored at a floor pivot behind and between the athlete's feet; the front end is loaded with plates clear of the floor. With knees bent and torso hinged forward, the athlete holds a double D-handle / V-handle cable attachment hooked under the bar shaft just behind the plates with both hands (palms facing each other) on straight arms.
  - `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/main/exercises/Bent_Over_Two-Arm_Long_Bar_Row/1.jpg`: The athlete pulls the loaded end of the bar upward with both arms, elbows tucked close to the sides and rising past the torso until the plates reach the lower chest, with the torso hinged forward and the knees bent in the same posture shown in `0.jpg` (two still photographs show the start and top postures, while keeping the torso still throughout the movement is required by the map's `"Quiet hinged torso"` family constraint rather than established by the two stills).

### Resolution of Hold Reasons & Choices
- **Pivot and anchor resolved**: One end of an Olympic barbell is anchored on the floor (in a corner, landmine pivot, or against a heavy floor stop) behind and between the athlete's feet so it cannot slide backward, and only the front end is loaded with plates. No cable stack is used; reading the catalogue's `"cable_machine"` equipment tag as referring to the double-handle cable attachment hooked under the bar shaft is the chosen interpretation (rather than verified catalogue provenance), and keeping the torso still throughout the pull and return comes from `ALL_300_MOTION_MAP.json`'s quiet-torso constraint (`"Quiet hinged torso"`), not from the two still photographs.
- **Two-arm grip resolved**: Sentence 3 contains an upstream copy-paste contradiction (`"grab the bar with both arms just behind the plates ... and put your other hand on your knee"`, carried over from a one-arm row entry). Both the movement name (`Bent Over Two-Arm Long Bar Row`), sentence 4 (`"Pull the bar straight up with your elbows in"`), sentence 4's Tip (`"Use a stirrup or double handle cable attachment by hooking it under the end of the bar"`), and both photographs (`0.jpg`, `1.jpg`) confirm that both hands hold the bar/handle throughout the movement and neither hand rests on a knee.
- **Marked as a choice**:
  1. **Double handle hooked under the bar with palms facing each other vs bare-bar grip, and the `"cable_machine"` tag reading (a choice)**: Sentence 3 says `"grab the bar with both arms just behind the plates"`, while sentence 4's Tip says `"Use a stirrup or double handle cable attachment by hooking it under the end of the bar"` and both photographs (`0.jpg`, `1.jpg`) show a double handle hooked under the bar just behind the plates with the palms facing each other. We choose the double handle hooked under the bar just behind the plates with the palms facing each other, and we mark reading the catalogue's `"cable_machine"` tag as that double-handle cable attachment (with no cable stack) as the chosen interpretation.
  2. **Torso angle and quiet-torso constraint (a choice)**: Sentence 2 says `"Bend forward until your torso is as close to parallel with the floor as you can"`, while `0.jpg` and `1.jpg` show the torso hinged forward at roughly 35–40 degrees above horizontal so the arms can hang straight without the plates hitting the floor. We state that the athlete bends forward from the hips with a flat back until the torso is near parallel to the floor (high enough that the plates stay clear of the floor at full arm extension), and we attribute `"the torso stays still"` to `ALL_300_MOTION_MAP.json`'s `"Quiet hinged torso"` family constraint rather than to the two still photographs.

### Draft Description (Authority for `145`)
- **Basis**: `Draft written from the public-domain free-exercise-db entry (Bent_Over_Two-Arm_Long_Bar_Row) and its two photographs, awaiting the owner. The catalogue's own text for this movement is a placeholder on a source hold.`
- **Instructions**:
  `Anchor one end of a barbell on the floor in a corner or landmine pivot so it cannot slide backward, load the other end with plates, and straddle the bar with the feet shoulder-width apart and the knees slightly bent. Bend forward from the hips with a flat back until the torso is near parallel to the floor, hook a double handle under the bar just behind the plates, and hold it with both hands, palms facing each other and arms straight. Keeping the torso still and the elbows tucked in close to your sides, pull the bar straight up until the plates touch your lower chest and hold for a second. Slowly lower the bar back to straight arms without letting the plates touch the floor.`
- **Cues**:
  `Back flat, torso still. Pull with the elbows tucked in until the plates touch the lower chest. Hold for a second, then lower slowly without touching the floor.`
- **Coaching Intent**:
  `Train the middle back, lats and biceps by rowing the loaded end of a floor-anchored barbell with both hands.`

## 2. Movement `153` — `Box Squat with Bands` (`box-squat-with-bands`)

### Hold Reason & Family Constraints
- **`MOV-A15.md` hold reason**: `band direction, implement, box contact and pause undefined`
- **`ALL_300_MOTION_MAP.json` `motion.source_hold_reason`**: `"Band direction, implement, box contact and pause undefined; current equipment includes barbell plus dumbbells."`
- **`ALL_300_MOTION_MAP.json` `motion.family_constraints`**: `"Box contact is deliberate seated pause, trunk stays braced. Bands must attach to actual ground/top anchors; 275 needs fast ascent and controlled descent."`

### Public-Domain Source Quotations & Photographs
- **JSON Source URL**: `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/main/dist/exercises.json` (entry `"id": "Box_Squat_with_Bands"`)
- **Word-for-Word `instructions`**:
  1. `"Begin in a power rack with a box at the appropriate height behind you. Set up the bands on the sleeves, secured to either band pegs, the rack, or dumbbells so that there is appropriate tension. If dumbbells are used, secure them so that they don't move. Also, ensure that the dumbbells you are using are heavy enough for the bands that you are using. Additional plates can be used to hold the dumbbells down. If more tension is needed, you can either widen the base on the floor or choke the bands. Typically, you would aim for a box height that brings you to a parallel squat, but you can train higher or lower if desired."`
  2. `"Begin by stepping under the bar and placing it across the back of the shoulders. Squeeze your shoulder blades together and rotate your elbows forward, attempting to bend the bar across your shoulders. Remove the bar from the rack, creating a tight arch in your lower back, and step back into position. Place your feet wider for more emphasis on the back, glutes, adductors, and hamstrings, or closer together for more quad development. Keep your head facing forward."`
  3. `"With your back, shoulders, and core tight, push your knees and butt out and you begin your descent. Sit back with your hips until you are seated on the box. Ideally, your shins should be perpendicular to the ground. Pause when you reach the box, and relax the hip flexors. Never bounce off of a box."`
  4. `"Keeping the weight on your heels and pushing your feet and knees out, drive upward off of the box as you lead the movement with your head. Continue upward, maintaining tightness head to toe. Use care to return the barbell to the rack."`
- **Photographs**:
  - `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/main/exercises/Box_Squat_with_Bands/0.jpg`: Side view of the athlete standing tall inside a power rack with a barbell across the back of the shoulders and a wooden box directly behind the athlete. Resistance bands run straight down from each barbell sleeve toward the bottom of the power rack, though the floor attachment itself is cropped at the bottom edge of `0.jpg`.
  - `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/main/exercises/Box_Squat_with_Bands/1.jpg`: The athlete sits back onto the box with the thighs about parallel to the floor, shins slightly inclined near vertical, back braced and head facing forward, while the bands remain taut between the barbell sleeves and the lower area of the rack (where the exact floor attachment is partly obscured).

### Resolution of Hold Reasons & Choices
- **Implement resolved**: A barbell held across the back of the shoulders inside a power rack (dumbbells in the catalogue's equipment list are only an optional floor anchor for the lower ends of the bands when band pegs or rack base rails are not used).
- **Band direction and anchor resolved**: A band loops from each sleeve of the barbell straight down to a floor anchor (band pegs/base of the power rack, or heavy dumbbells secured on the floor), pulling downward and increasing tension as the athlete stands.
- **Box contact, trunk bracing, and pause resolved**: Keeping the trunk (abdomen and back) braced through the descent, the seated pause, and the drive up, the athlete sits back with the hips until seated on the parallel-height box with shins approximately vertical, feet planted, pauses on the box (`"Pause when you reach the box, and relax the hip flexors. Never bounce off of a box"`), and then drives upward off the box through the heels while keeping whole-foot pressure.
- **Marked as a choice**:
  1. **Floor anchor (rack base/band pegs vs floor dumbbells, a choice)**: Sentence 1 allows the bands to be `"secured to either band pegs, the rack, or dumbbells"`, whereas `0.jpg` crops the floor attachment and `1.jpg` partly obscures the exact lower attachment. We name both in the setup sentence and choose anchoring the bands to the base of the rack at floor level as **a choice** from sentence 1 (not as something proven by both photographs).
  2. **Box height, shins approximately vertical, and stance width in the instructions (a choice)**: Sentence 1 states `"Typically, you would aim for a box height that brings you to a parallel squat, but you can train higher or lower if desired"`, sentence 2 allows a wider or closer stance (which the side-view photographs do not establish), and sentence 3 calls perpendicular shins an ideal (`"Ideally, your shins should be perpendicular to the ground"`, while `1.jpg` shows slightly inclined shins). Choosing a parallel-height box, stating `"shins approximately vertical, feet planted"` instead of requiring strictly perpendicular shins, and putting the chosen shoulder-width to slightly wider stance directly in the instructions are marked as **a choice**.

### Draft Description (Authority for `153`)
- **Basis**: `Draft written from the public-domain free-exercise-db entry (Box_Squat_with_Bands) and its two photographs, awaiting the owner. The catalogue's own text for this movement is a placeholder on a source hold.`
- **Instructions**:
  `Set a parallel-height box behind you inside a power rack and loop a band from each sleeve of a barbell down to the base of the rack or heavy dumbbells secured on the floor. Step under the bar to set it across the back of your shoulders, unrack it with your trunk braced and your head facing forward, and stand in front of the box in a shoulder-width to slightly wider stance with your feet planted. Keeping your trunk braced through the descent, push your hips back and knees out to sit back under control until you are seated on the box with your thighs parallel to the floor and your shins approximately vertical, feet planted. Pause on the box without bouncing while keeping your trunk braced, then drive upward off the box through your heels with whole-foot pressure to stand tall.`
- **Cues**:
  `Keep the trunk braced through the descent, the seated pause and the drive up. Sit back until you are seated on the box with shins approximately vertical, feet planted, and pause without bouncing. Drive up through your heels over the whole foot against the bands.`
- **Coaching Intent**:
  `Train the quadriceps, glutes and hamstrings with a paused parallel box squat against downward band tension.`

## 3. Movement `165` — `Calf Raise On A Dumbbell` (`calf-raise-on-a-dumbbell`)

### Hold Reason & Family Constraints
- **`MOV-A15.md` hold reason**: `dumbbell as a foot platform versus a held load, support and stance undefined`
- **`ALL_300_MOTION_MAP.json` `motion.source_hold_reason`**: `"Dumbbell-as-foot-platform versus held dumbbell load, support and stance undefined."`
- **`ALL_300_MOTION_MAP.json` `motion.family_constraints`**: `"Forefoot stays on support and heel moves via ankle; quiet knees/hips, top pause, controlled heel return. 165 support on versus load in hands remains unresolved."`

### Public-Domain Source Quotations & Photographs
- **JSON Source URL**: `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/main/dist/exercises.json` (entry `"id": "Calf_Raise_On_A_Dumbbell"`)
- **Word-for-Word `instructions`**:
  1. `"Hang on to a sturdy object for balance and stand on a dumbbell handle, preferably one with round plates so that it rolls as in this manner you have to work harder to stabilize yourself; thus increasing the effectiveness of the exercise."`
  2. `"Now roll your foot slightly forward so that you can get a nice stretch of the calf. This will be your starting position."`
  3. `"Lift the calf as you roll your foot over the top of the handle so that you get a full extension. Exhale during the execution of this movement. Contract the calf hard at the top and hold for a second. Tip: As you come up, roll the dumbbell slightly backward."`
  4. `"Now inhale as you roll the dumbbell slightly forward as you come down to get a better stretch."`
  5. `"Repeat for the recommended amount of repetitions."`
- **Photographs**:
  - `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/main/exercises/Calf_Raise_On_A_Dumbbell/0.jpg`: Rear view of the athlete facing a rack with a horizontal bar at hip height, holding that bar with both hands for balance (while a still photograph cannot by itself certify that the bar is fixed against movement or that the dumbbell does not roll between frames). A single dumbbell rests on the floor under the ball of the athlete's right foot; the right heel is lowered toward the floor for a calf stretch, while the left foot is held off the floor in the air.
  - `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/main/exercises/Calf_Raise_On_A_Dumbbell/1.jpg`: Holding the horizontal support bar with both hands and keeping the left foot off the floor, the athlete rises high onto the ball of the right foot on the dumbbell handle into full ankle extension with the right knee and hip straight.

### Resolution of Hold Reasons & Choices
- **Dumbbell as foot platform vs held load resolved**: Settled by sentence 1 (`"stand on a dumbbell handle"`) and both photographs (`0.jpg`, `1.jpg`): the dumbbell sits on the floor as the elevated foot platform under the ball of the working foot; no dumbbell is held in the hands.
- **Support resolved**: Settled by sentence 1 (`"Hang on to a sturdy object for balance"`) and both photographs showing two-hand support: both hands hold a sturdy support that cannot move in front of the athlete at waist/hip height.
- **Stance resolved**: Sentences 2–3 use the singular (`"roll your foot"`, `"Lift the calf as you roll your foot over the top of the handle"`), a dumbbell handle fits one foot, and both photographs (`0.jpg`, `1.jpg`) show a single-leg stance with the ball of one foot on the dumbbell handle and the non-working foot lifted off the floor.
- **Marked as a choice**:
  1. **Deliberate safety change from the source's rolling dumbbell to a non-rolling dumbbell and fixed support (a choice)**: Sentence 1 says `"preferably one with round plates so that it rolls"` and sentences 3–4 explicitly prescribe rolling the dumbbell backward as you rise and forward as you lower (`"Tip: As you come up, roll the dumbbell slightly backward"`, `"roll the dumbbell slightly forward as you come down"`), and two still photographs cannot prove that the dumbbell stayed stationary between frames. Because balancing on one foot on a narrow rolling handle creates a material slip and ankle-roll hazard, we make a deliberate safety change from the source's rolling version (marked as **a choice**, not as something the photographs show): use a dumbbell that cannot roll (hexagonal heads, or wedged), check it is stable before stepping on, and hold a support that cannot move; the dumbbell stays still while the ankle lowers and rises, and the athlete changes legs after completing the repetitions.

### Draft Description (Authority for `165`)
- **Basis**: `Draft written from the public-domain free-exercise-db entry (Calf_Raise_On_A_Dumbbell) and its two photographs, with a deliberate safety change replacing the source's rolling dumbbell with a stationary non-rolling dumbbell, awaiting the owner. The catalogue's own text for this movement is a placeholder on a source hold.`
- **Instructions**:
  `Use a dumbbell that cannot roll (hexagonal heads, or wedged) placed on the floor in front of a sturdy support that cannot move, check it is stable before stepping on, hold the support with both hands for balance, and stand with the ball of one foot on the dumbbell handle and the other foot lifted clear of the floor. Keeping the knee straight and the dumbbell still, lower the working heel toward the floor to get a full stretch of the calf. While the dumbbell stays still, lift the heel to rise on the ball of the foot over the handle into a full calf extension, contract the calf hard at the top, and hold for a second. Lower the heel under control back into the stretch while the dumbbell stays still, complete the repetitions, then change legs.`
- **Cues**:
  `Use a dumbbell that cannot roll, check it is stable before stepping on, and hold a support that cannot move. Keep the dumbbell still while the ankle lowers and rises into full extension and holds for a second. Lower the heel under control into a stretch with the knee straight.`
- **Coaching Intent**:
  `Train the calf one leg at a time through a full stretch and contraction by standing on the handle of a stable, non-rolling dumbbell on the floor.`

## 4. Movement `205` — `Front Barbell Squat To A Bench` (`front-barbell-squat-to-a-bench`)

### Hold Reason & Family Constraints
- **`MOV-A15.md` hold reason**: `front rack, bench height, light touch versus seated pause absent`
- **`ALL_300_MOTION_MAP.json` `motion.source_hold_reason`**: `"Front rack, bench height and light touch versus seated pause absent from current generic source."`
- **`ALL_300_MOTION_MAP.json` `motion.family_constraints`**: `"Front rack and bench contact need an explicit touch versus seated-pause decision before authoring."`

### Public-Domain Source Quotations & Photographs
- **JSON Source URL**: `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/main/dist/exercises.json` (entry `"id": "Front_Barbell_Squat_To_A_Bench"`)
- **Word-for-Word `instructions`**:
  1. `"This exercise is best performed inside a squat rack for safety purposes. To begin, first set a flat bench behind you and set the bar on a rack that best matches your height. Once the correct height is chosen and the bar is loaded, bring your arms up under the bar while keeping the elbows high and the upper arm slightly above parallel to the floor. Rest the bar on top of the deltoids and cross your arms while grasping the bar for total control."`
  2. `"Lift the bar off the rack by first pushing with your legs and at the same time straightening your torso."`
  3. `"Step away from the rack and position your legs using a shoulder width medium stance with the toes slightly pointed out. Keep your head up at all times as looking down will get you off balance and also maintain a straight back. This will be your starting position. (Note: For the purposes of this discussion we will use the medium stance described above which targets overall development; however you can choose any of the three stances described in the foot positioning section)."`
  4. `"Begin to slowly lower the bar by bending the knees as you maintain a straight posture with the head up. Continue down until you touch the bench with your glutes. Inhale as you perform this portion of the movement. Tip: If you performed the exercise correctly, the front of the knees should make an imaginary straight line with the toes that is perpendicular to the front. If your knees are past that imaginary line (if they are past your toes) then you are placing undue stress on the knee and the exercise has been performed incorrectly."`
  5. `"Begin to raise the bar as you exhale by pushing the floor mainly with the heel of your foot as you straighten the legs again and go back to the starting position."`
  6. `"Repeat for the recommended amount of repetitions."`
- **Photographs**:
  - `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/main/exercises/Front_Barbell_Squat_To_A_Bench/0.jpg`: Rear-oblique view of the athlete standing tall inside a squat rack in a shoulder-width stance in front of a knee-high bench/stool with a plate on it. The barbell rests on top of the front deltoids with the arms crossed in front grasping the bar and the upper arms held slightly above parallel to the floor.
  - `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/main/exercises/Front_Barbell_Squat_To_A_Bench/1.jpg`: The athlete squats down with an upright back and high elbows until the athlete's glutes contact the top of the bench/platform at roughly parallel thigh depth, with the knees tracking in the direction of the toes (driving back up through the heels is prescribed by sentence 5 of the source text rather than visible motion in the still photograph).

### Resolution of Hold Reasons & Choices
- **Front rack resolved**: Settled by sentence 1 (`"bring your arms up under the bar while keeping the elbows high and the upper arm slightly above parallel to the floor. Rest the bar on top of the deltoids and cross your arms while grasping the bar for total control"`) and both photographs (`0.jpg`, `1.jpg`): a cross-arm front rack across the top of the front shoulders with the upper arms slightly above parallel to the floor.
- **Light touch vs seated pause resolved**: Settled by sentences 4 and 5 (`"Continue down until you touch the bench with your glutes"` followed immediately by `"Begin to raise the bar as you exhale by pushing the floor mainly with the heel of your foot"`): unlike `153` (`Box Squat with Bands`, which specifies `"Sit back with your hips until you are seated on the box ... Pause when you reach the box"`), `205` lowers until the glutes touch the bench and then drives back up without sitting back to relax on the bench.
- **Marked as a choice**:
  1. **Bench height carried into the instructions (a choice)**: Sentence 1 says `"set a flat bench behind you"` without specifying a height, while `1.jpg` shows a stool/platform with a plate on it where the thighs reach roughly parallel to the floor at contact and cannot prescribe one numeric height for all athletes. Using `"a stable bench set so the glutes touch it lightly at about thighs parallel"` — and carrying that bench-height choice directly into the instructions — is marked as **a choice**.
  2. **Brief touch without unloading (a choice)**: Because sentences 4–5 say `"until you touch the bench with your glutes"` and `"Begin to raise the bar"` (and a still photograph alone cannot distinguish a brief touch from an unloaded seated pause), touching the bench lightly with the glutes under control without sitting and relaxing on it before driving up is marked as **a choice**.
  3. **Omitting the dated knee-past-toes tip and cueing knees tracking with the toes over a whole foot (a choice)**: Sentence 4's Tip claims that if the knees pass a vertical line from the toes `"you are placing undue stress on the knee and the exercise has been performed incorrectly"`. Because in an upright front squat to a bench the knees normally travel forward over the toes while tracking in the direction of the toes, and artificially restricting anterior knee travel redistributes load toward the hips and increases trunk lean (cf. Fry et al., 2003, PMID 14636100), that dated restriction is deliberately left out of the draft instructions as an intentional departure from the historical source, and `"knees tracking with the toes over a whole foot"` is taught instead.

### Draft Description (Authority for `205`)
- **Basis**: `Draft written from the public-domain free-exercise-db entry (Front_Barbell_Squat_To_A_Bench) and its two photographs, omitting the source's dated knee-past-toes restriction, awaiting the owner. The catalogue's own text for this movement is a placeholder on a source hold.`
- **Instructions**:
  `Inside a squat rack, place a stable bench set so the glutes touch it lightly at about thighs parallel behind you, rest a barbell across the top of your front shoulders with your arms crossed to grasp the bar and your upper arms slightly above parallel to the floor, and step back into a shoulder-width stance with the toes slightly pointed out. Keeping your head up, your back straight, and your elbows high, bend the knees tracking with the toes over a whole foot to lower slowly until your glutes lightly touch the bench at about thighs parallel. Do not sit back and relax on the bench. Push the floor through your heels over the whole foot to straighten the legs and return to standing tall.`
- **Cues**:
  `Elbows high, upper arms slightly above parallel. Lower slowly with a straight back and knees tracking with the toes over a whole foot until your glutes lightly touch the bench at about thighs parallel. Drive through your heels over the whole foot to stand right back up.`
- **Coaching Intent**:
  `Train the quadriceps and glutes with a cross-arm front squat to a controlled light touch at about thighs parallel on a bench behind you.`
