## 3. Contract shape is right

Seven XFAIL — the six stationary-template cycles plus nominal gain-3 upward
saturation — matches the C4 order exactly. Both zero-noise stationary
counterexamples are included, which is what makes this robust: no noise seed to
hide behind.

Four expected-PASS: paired healthy/niggle override, carry thin-data, rotation
thin-data, and double-run determinism. The override case going in as
expected-PASS is the important one — it is the safety property that currently
works, and it now cannot silently regress while everything around it is
rewritten.

**The XPASS rule is the best design decision here.** An unexpected pass fails the
gate until deliberately converted. That means when R1 or R2 fixes a
counterexample, the gate goes red and forces an explicit decision rather than
quietly turning green. It closes the loop in both directions, which is precisely
what was missing from the original `verify:autopilot` — that gate could not
detect a wrong λ because its pins were λ-blind.

Naming was taken seriously: `verify:autopilot-counterexamples`, not a stability
gate. Nothing in the name claims a property it does not test.

## 5. Standing back

The chain that produced this started as "should I adopt this tool." What it
actually produced is a machine-encoded, deterministic, zero-noise counterexample
set proving a live defect in shipped code — that a healthy athlete with no niggle
is ratcheted toward `base_rpe_cap` on a trend signal biased by deload placement,
with no gate and no UI attribution.

The tool did not produce that. The **ratification checkpoints** did: C2 caught a
derivation that would have anchored the sweep to a wrong boundary, and C3's
per-block action tables caught a saturation that every variance-based test in the
chain reported as convergence. Keep the checkpoints; they are the reusable part.
